  <p><strong>SEO, AEO and GEO share one foundation: a site that is crawlable, clear and credible.</strong> They differ in what is observed (rankings vs answers), what is measured, and which access policies apply. You do not need three strategies; you need one strategy with three measurement layers.</p>
</div>

Every few years the discipline acquires a new acronym. The rebranding is mostly packaging. The work underneath has stable parts and genuinely new parts, and confusing the two wastes budget.

## The comparison

| Dimension          | SEO                     | AEO (answer engines)           | GEO (generative engines)       |
| ------------------ | ----------------------- | ------------------------------ | ------------------------------ |
| Surface            | Search results pages    | Assistants with citations      | Generative answers in chat     |
| Unit of visibility | Position + snippet      | Mention + cited link           | Mention inside the answer      |
| Access requirement | Crawl + index           | Crawl policy + content clarity | Crawl policy + content clarity |
| Observation        | Stable-ish SERP tooling | Sampled, dated runs            | Sampled, dated runs            |
| Volatility         | Moderate                | High (model + prompt variance) | High                           |
| Guarantee possible | No                      | No                             | No                             |

Google's own guidance for AI features describes the same fundamentals: unique, people-first content, clear structure and crawl access.

## What genuinely changes

- **Observation method:** rankings were toolable; answer visibility needs [sampled, dated baselines](/insights/build-ai-visibility-baseline/).
- **Access policies:** AI crawlers have their own robots tokens and policies, separate from Googlebot. The [crawler-policy article](/insights/ai-crawler-access-policy/) covers the decision matrix.
- **Unit of success:** a mention without a link can still be commercial visibility; define and count it deliberately.

## What does not change

- Crawlable architecture and clean internal linking.
- Content that states facts clearly, with structure a machine can quote.
- Entity clarity: consistent naming, about pages, external corroboration.
- Honesty about measurement limits.

## The work structure

1. One content strategy: facts, structure, sources.
2. One access policy: decide which crawlers may fetch what.
3. Three measurement layers: rankings where toolable, sampled answer baselines, referral/CRM joins.

For the measurement layer design, review the [measurement foundation](/services/measurement-foundation/) service.
