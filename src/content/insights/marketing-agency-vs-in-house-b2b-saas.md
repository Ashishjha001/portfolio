Most "agency vs in-house" content is written by agencies arguing for agencies, or by payroll-tool vendors arguing for hires. Both compare a full marketing team against a full-service retainer — a comparison almost no early-stage SaaS actually faces.

The real decision has more options than two:

1. One senior in-house marketer, roughly $215k per year fully loaded in the US
2. A scoped agency or consultancy engagement, $30k to $180k per year per published ranges
3. A fractional or scoped specialist on a project or diagnostic basis
4. Founder-led, hiring nobody yet

This article compares all four, including the option most content ignores.

## The 24-month in-house math

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics reports a median wage for marketing managers of **$166,790 per year** (May 2025), with the lowest 10 percent under $90,260 and the highest 10 percent above $293,610. Wages are only part of the cost: BLS Employer Costs for Employee Compensation (March 2026) shows benefits averaging **30.1 percent of total compensation** for private-industry workers.

That makes one fully loaded senior marketer approximately:

| Component                                                      | Annual cost |
| -------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------- |
| Median wage, marketing manager (BLS, May 2025)                 | $166,790    |
| Benefits at 30.1% of total compensation (BLS ECEC, March 2026) | ~$50,300    |
| Fully loaded, before tools and recruiting                      | ~$217,000   |

Add the tooling an independent marketer needs (analytics, SEO and automation subscriptions, commonly hundreds to a few thousand dollars monthly), recruiting cost (often 15 to 25 percent of first-year salary via agency hires, or founder-hours otherwise), and the two to four months a new hire typically takes to become productive.

**The 24-month cost of one hire: roughly $435,000 to $460,000**, of which the first months produce little output.

Stated plainly: these are US national medians. Indian and European salaries are materially different, and your market's rates apply. A junior hire costs less and delivers less; a head of marketing costs more. The number is a planning anchor, not a quote.

## The 24-month agency math

Agency pricing is less standardized, but several agencies publish ranges (verified at source, September 2026):

- TripleDart, a SaaS-specialist agency, publishes retainers from **$3,500 per month** for SEO and content work, within a general SaaS retainer range of **$900 to $20,000+ per month** by scope.
- SaaS Hero publishes flat retainers from **$1,250 per month** for single-channel work to **$7,000+ per month** for a full marketing team tier.
- Agency roundups commonly cite **$3,000 to $15,000 per month** for small-to-mid B2B SaaS retainers.

These are vendor-published figures. Treat them as market context, not benchmarks. The full picture adds ad spend (usually separate and often the larger number) and your own management time — briefing, reviewing, aligning — realistically two to six hours weekly.

**The 24-month agency cost at a $5,000 monthly retainer: $120,000 plus your time plus ad spend.**

## What the money buys, and what it cannot

The real trade is only partially about cost.

In-house wins when brand and product knowledge compounds, marketing is a continuous daily function, and the work is mostly execution you can supervise.

Agency or consultancy wins when the need is scoped or specialized — an SEO rebuild, a measurement fix, a launch — speed matters (weeks versus a hiring cycle), and you need senior-level judgment without senior-level headcount.

Neither wins when you do not yet know what marketing your product needs. Paying $10k per month to an agency, or hiring anyone, before the question is framed mostly buys activity rather than answers. That is the situation where a scoped diagnostic, or simply more founder-led selling, is the honest recommendation.

## The decision framework

Use this sequence in order. Stop at the first "no."

1. **Do you know what marketing problem you are solving?** If no, do not hire anyone. Run founder-led discovery first.
2. **Is the work continuous and execution-heavy?** Yes leans in-house. No leans scoped external.
3. **Is the work specialized beyond generalist skills** — measurement, AI-search visibility, technical SEO? Yes leans external specialist.
4. **Can you supervise quality** — brief, review, judge output? If no, hire in-house senior you trust, or an external with fixed-scope deliverables you can evaluate.
5. **Run the 24-month numbers** for your actual candidates. The decision makes itself.
