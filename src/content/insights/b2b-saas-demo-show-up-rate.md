The honest answer is: there is no single show-up benchmark you should trust without seeing the denominator.

Some reports measure booked meetings. Others measure meetings whose scheduled time has already passed. Some count cancellations as failures; others remove them. A company with a 90% “show rate” may simply be excluding difficult records.

This is the final stage in the [Demand-to-Demo diagnostic framework](/insights/traffic-up-demo-bookings-down/), so the denominator must include only meetings that were actually due to occur.

## Define the metric before benchmarking it

Use:

> **Demo show-up rate = held qualified demos ÷ demos due to occur**

For a clean calculation:

- Exclude meetings scheduled for the future.
- Count a rescheduled meeting once, according to its final outcome.
- Report cancellations separately from no-shows.
- Remove internal tests and obvious spam.
- Keep inbound, outbound, partner and customer meetings separate.

A RevenueHero snapshot of 6,428 scheduled meetings reported a 6.5% no-show rate, but only a 76.1% completion rate because the dataset also contained meetings in other statuses. That difference shows why “no-show rate” and “show-up rate” are not always perfect opposites.

## Build your own useful benchmark

Start with your trailing 90 days and segment by:

- Lead source
- ICP tier
- Region and time zone
- Days between booking and meeting
- Assigned rep
- Instant booking versus manual scheduling
- New prospect versus existing account

Then compare each segment with itself over time.

A 72% show-up rate may be concerning for high-intent inbound demos booked the same day. It may be strong for cold outbound meetings scheduled two weeks ahead. Without context, the percentage says very little.

## Diagnose the loss after booking

If show-up rate falls, check:

1. Was the meeting booked too far into the future?
2. Did the page promise something different from the calendar invitation?
3. Did the prospect receive a clear agenda and reminder?
4. Can they reschedule without starting again?
5. Are low-fit leads booking because qualification rules weakened?
6. Does one source, region or representative explain most of the decline?

The real goal is not the highest possible show rate. You could achieve that by making it extremely difficult to book.

The goal is **more qualified meetings held**, with low friction for the right buyers.

Track show-up rate beside demo-to-opportunity conversion. If attendance rises while opportunity quality collapses, the funnel has not improved.
