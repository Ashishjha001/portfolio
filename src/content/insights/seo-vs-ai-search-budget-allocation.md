  <p><strong>Do not begin with a fixed SEO-versus-AI-search percentage.</strong> Fund the shared technical, content, source and measurement foundations first. Then place the next increment against the best-evidenced bottleneck: conventional search discovery, generated-answer presence, third-party source coverage or the path from either surface into pipeline.</p>

This guide is for B2B SaaS founders, CMOs, SEO leaders and demand-generation teams deciding whether to move money from conventional SEO into AEO, GEO or AI-search visibility work.

Current search results often recommend a percentage split. A default percentage is easy to repeat and difficult to defend. It rarely knows whether the site is indexable, whether commercial pages exist, whether buyers use answer surfaces for this category, whether the company appears in the observed sample or whether either channel connects to revenue.

Make the decision about the **next ₹ or next team-month**, not the label on an annual budget line.

## Most of the foundation is shared

Google’s current [generative AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide?hl=en) says its AI features use core Search ranking and quality systems, and that established SEO practices remain relevant. It also advises against unnecessary AI text files and other purported shortcuts for Google’s generative features.

The shared work includes:

- crawlable, indexable and usable pages;
- clear page ownership and internal links;
- original, maintained information;
- accurate entities, products, people and organization details;
- credible external sources that can corroborate claims;
- measurement that distinguishes impressions, mentions, citations, referrals and pipeline;
- explicit update dates and limitations where facts can change.

Calling shared work “SEO” in one budget and “GEO” in another does not create two independent growth systems.

The investments diverge at the observation and repair layer.

| Investment layer    | Conventional search emphasis                           | AI-search emphasis                                                            |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Demand observation  | Queries, impressions, clicks, pages                    | Buyer-question panel, mentions, citations, answer sources                     |
| Technical diagnosis | Crawl, render, index, canonical, result presentation   | Surface-specific access, retrieval, source and answer checks                  |
| Content repair      | Page intent, evidence, internal links, conversion path | Self-contained answers, source clarity, entity consistency, quotable evidence |
| External evidence   | Links, references, reputation                          | Sources used or cited across observed answers                                 |
| Measurement         | Search Console, analytics, CRM                         | Surface log, Google AI report where available, referrals, CRM                 |

## Use the SHARED–SEARCH–ANSWER–REVENUE framework

Score the decision across four layers before allocating money.

### SHARED: Are the foundations functional?

- Important pages are crawlable and indexable.
- Each commercial need has a clear page owner.
- Claims have maintained evidence.
- Internal links expose priority resources.
- Analytics and CRM retain a defensible source path.

If these fail, the first allocation is shared foundation work.

### SEARCH: Is conventional search the constrained discovery path?

- Buyers search for the category, use case, integration or comparison.
- Relevant pages do not exist or do not receive impressions.
- Rankings or clicks reach the wrong page.
- Organic visitors arrive but do not continue into an appropriate action.

If these are the clearest losses, fund the SEO-specific repair.

### ANSWER: Is generated-answer visibility the constrained evaluation path?

- A defined buyer-question sample shows competitors but not the company.
- The company is mentioned without a useful citation or is described inaccurately.
- Recurring third-party sources omit or misclassify the company.
- Relevant pages are accessible but do not contain the evidence needed to answer the question.

If these patterns repeat in a dated sample, fund the AI-search-specific observation and repair.

### REVENUE: Can either path change a commercial decision?

- The team can distinguish an observed referral from an inferred influence.
- Conversions reach the CRM with stage definitions intact.
- The affected questions matter to selection, evaluation or purchase.
- The proposed work has an owner, expected signal and review date.

If revenue joins are broken, measurement may be the highest-value allocation even when visibility is weak.

## Score readiness before scoring opportunity

Use 0, 1 or 2 for each condition.

| Condition                    | 0                                  | 1                                 | 2                                |
| ---------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | --------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Technical foundation         | Important pages blocked or missing | Partial                           | Verified                         |
| Commercial page coverage     | Major decisions have no owner      | Mixed                             | Clear ownership                  |
| Conventional search evidence | No usable data                     | Directional                       | Stable query-page evidence       |
| AI-surface evidence          | Screenshots or anecdotes           | Small repeatable panel            | Dated, surface-specific baseline |
| Source evidence              | Unknown                            | Some recurring sources identified | Source gaps mapped               |
| Revenue measurement          | No join                            | Partial referral or CRM join      | Tested path with limitations     |

Do not reward the newer channel for having less evidence. A low AI-evidence score means “build a baseline,” not “assign a large optimization budget.” The [AI visibility baseline guide](/insights/build-ai-visibility-baseline/) shows how to collect a fixed question panel.

Download the [SEO and AI-search allocation scorecard](/downloads/seo-ai-search-allocation-scorecard.csv) to record evidence, readiness, expected signal, owner and stopping rule.

## Choose from five allocation decisions

### 1. Repair the shared foundation

Use when access, page ownership, evidence quality or measurement is broken. This work should benefit both discovery paths.

### 2. Fund conventional search depth

Use when commercial search demand is visible, the current owner is weak or missing, and the expected signal can be observed in queries, impressions, clicks or qualified paths.

### 3. Fund an AI-search baseline

Use when buyers plausibly use generated answers but the company has only screenshots or tool scores. Observation precedes optimization.

### 4. Repair answer-source gaps

Use when repeated observations show that relevant public sources omit necessary facts, or the owned page lacks clear evidence. Do not manufacture mentions or reviews.

### 5. Fund measurement before more visibility

Use when both search paths create activity but source, conversion and CRM records cannot support the next budget decision.

## DEMONSTRATION RUN

**Collection date:** 28 August 2026  
**Dataset or sample:** Synthetic readiness review for one hypothetical B2B SaaS site and a ₹10 lakh incremental search budget. No client, Data Decision Consulting performance or external benchmark is used.  
**Method:** Score six readiness conditions, identify the earliest constraint and assign money to shared, search-specific, answer-specific and measurement work.  
**Assumptions:** The site has stable conventional search impressions, incomplete commercial pages, a 40-question AI baseline with repeatable competitor mentions, and a partial CRM join.  
**Limitations:** Scores depend on judgement; the example does not estimate traffic, citations, pipeline or return.  
**What it can prove:** Whether the allocation follows the observed constraint and produces reviewable work.  
**What it cannot prove:** That the split is suitable for another company or that the spend will create a particular result.

| Work package                                    |         Amount | Expected signal                                                | Stop or review condition                                |
| ----------------------------------------------- | -------------: | -------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |

The example assigns 40% to shared page and link work because the observed page-ownership gap affects conventional and generated discovery. It assigns 20% to AI-specific work because the baseline is still shallow. Those percentages are consequences of the assumptions, not recommendations.

## Measure each surface with the metric it can support

Google is rolling out a separate [generative AI performance report](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16984139) in Search Console for AI Overviews and AI Mode. The documentation notes eligibility and rollout limits. Where available, use it for Google generative-feature impressions by page, country, date and device. Do not treat it as a cross-engine AI-visibility report.

Keep four evidence layers separate:

1. **Presence:** the company appeared in the defined answer sample.
2. **Citation:** an answer linked or attributed a supporting source.
3. **Referral:** an identifiable visit arrived from an answer surface.
4. **Pipeline:** an observable visit joined to a defined CRM outcome.

The [three-surface measurement guide](/insights/three-ai-surfaces-measurement/) explains why ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AI features should not be blended into one rank. The [GA4 AI-referral check](/insights/measuring-ai-referrals-ga4/) covers observable visits and missing referrers.

## Failure conditions

### A default split is presented as a benchmark

Reject it unless the sample, method, market and limitations are visible and comparable to the decision.

### Tool visibility becomes the business objective

A monitoring score can support diagnosis. It cannot establish revenue, causality or a universal rank.

### Shared work is charged twice

Keep one backlog for crawlability, page quality, internal links and maintained evidence. Tag which surfaces each task is expected to support.

### AI work creates duplicate pages for query variations

Google’s guidance warns against creating separate pages for every fan-out variation. Use the [query-to-page ownership method](/insights/b2b-saas-seo-query-to-page-map/) before adding a URL.

### Citations are pursued without evidence worth citing

Create or improve the underlying method, dataset, comparison, specification or limitation. Do not use fabricated research or inauthentic mentions.

### Direct traffic is relabelled as AI influence

Keep unknown journeys unknown. Qualitative buyer evidence can be recorded separately without rewriting the analytics source.

## Recommended next action

Run a two-hour evidence review. Mark every proposed task as shared, search-specific, answer-specific or measurement. Then score readiness and fund the earliest repair that can change a commercial decision. Review the allocation after the expected signal has had time to appear; do not roll it forward because the budget category already exists.
